## Standard And Product Decision

- Standard or product policy: duplicated operands around comparison, logical, subtraction, and division operators are suspicious and usually indicate copy-paste mistakes.
- Why this diagnostic exists: expressions like `A = A`, `A И A`, or `X / X` often hide an intended second operand.
- Non-goals: algebraic simplification, constant folding, harmless addition/multiplication duplicates, or full equivalence proving.
- Keep/drop decision: keep as a BSLLS-compatible suspicious-expression diagnostic.

## Semantic Oracle

- Checked language fact: a CST `binary_expression` has identical left/right operand text around a suspicious operator, or a logical chain repeats the same operand.
- Must diagnose when: identical normalized operands appear around `<`, `>`, `<=`, `>=`, `=`, `<>`, `И`/`And`, `ИЛИ`/`Or`, `-`, or `/`, except default popular divisors for division.
- Must not diagnose when: operands differ, the operator is `+` or `*`, the duplicate division operand is one of `60` or `1024`, or the expression is a simple truth literal condition rather than an identical binary expression.
- Diagnostic anchor token/range: full binary expression span.
- Quick-fix target, if any: replace one operand with the intended expression, remove the duplicate condition, or keep a documented popular divisor.

## CST Or Domain Fact Model

- Parser/tree source: tree-sitter CST walk over `binary_expression` nodes.
- CST/domain object types: `binary_expression`, `operator`, left/right operand nodes, and flattened logical operand chains.
- Extracted attributes: operator text, normalized operand keys, duplicate operand key, source range, and severity.
- Parse-error behavior: malformed expressions may be skipped; BSL001 owns syntax errors.
- Textual/regex behavior, only if genuinely textual: operand keys are normalized node text because the rule compares source-equivalent operand spelling, not evaluated values.
