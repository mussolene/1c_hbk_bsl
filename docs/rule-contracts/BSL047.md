## Standard And Product Decision

- Standard or product policy: meaningful boundary dates in executable code should be named or isolated instead of hidden as unexplained literals.
- Why this diagnostic exists: unexplained date constants make business rules harder to review and safely change.
- Non-goals: authorized platform sentinel dates, comments, invalid date-looking strings, simple constant declarations, return constants, structure/map initializers, or broader magic-number detection.
- Keep/drop decision: keep as a BSLLS-compatible magic-date diagnostic.

## Semantic Oracle

- Checked language fact: a date-like BSL date/string literal is used directly inside an executable expression.
- Must diagnose when: a non-authorized date-like literal participates in an expression, condition, function argument, ternary branch, or date constructor expression where the date meaning is not isolated as a simple constant.
- Must not diagnose when: the literal is in comments, authorized by `authorizedDates`, invalid as a date-like value, used as a simple assignment/return constant, appears in a method declaration line, or belongs to structure/map insertion contexts documented as exceptions.
- Diagnostic anchor token/range: date-like literal span including the surrounding quotes.
- Quick-fix target, if any: extract the date to a named constant/helper or add it to the allowed-date policy when it is a documented sentinel.
